Bowling for Beginners

This is the straightforward manual to becoming a better bowler. While many believe that bowling is all about using your strength to knock down ten pins with a ball, true professionals realize that it's much more than that. Bowling is a game where accurate aim, adaptability, proper strategy, and versatility play a greater role than pure strength. 20% of the game is physical and 80% is mental. On this page, we will focus on the basics of bowling which include selecting proper hook equipment, understanding lane oils, and utilizing target systems. If you want to improve your mental game, you found the perfect page!

Understanding the Lane

Bowling lanes are made up of 40 individual boards that bowlers use to line up their feet and visualize a trajectory of where they throw the ball. There are also arrows farther down the lane that act as targets, so bowlers usually aim at an arrow/board instead of staring directly at the pins. As you progress, you'll then begin visualizing two things for your release: the arrow that you throw at about 15 feet away and your breakpoint which is where the ball begins to hook toward the center of the lane. The pins are arranged in a triangle, with the 1-pin in front and the others numbered behind it. Every miss of the pocket is data and by keeping the same starting position and aiming at the same target, a bowler can tell whether a missed shot came from their release, direction, or ball movement. You can then make small adjustments with their feet or target to improve the next shot.
